# How Much Should a Small Business Set Aside for Taxes?

One of the most common questions we hear from new business owners is: "How much should I be setting aside for taxes?" The answer isn't one-size-fits-all, but there are proven frameworks that work.

## The Quick Answer

**Most small business owners should set aside 25-30% of net profit for federal and state taxes.** This covers:
- Federal income tax (10-37% depending on bracket)
- Self-employment tax (15.3% on net earnings)
- State income tax (varies by state)

## Breaking Down the Tax Burden

### Self-Employment Tax: The Hidden Cost
If you're a sole proprietor, LLC member, or partner, you pay **15.3% self-employment tax** on your net business income. This covers:
- 12.4% for Social Security (on first $168,600 in 2024)
- 2.9% for Medicare (no income limit)
- Additional 0.9% Medicare tax on income over $200,000

### Federal Income Tax
Your business income is taxed at your personal tax rate:
- 10% on income up to $11,600 (single)
- 12% on income $11,601 to $47,150
- 22% on income $47,151 to $100,525
- And higher brackets for higher earners

### State Income Tax
Varies by state. New York, for example, ranges from 4% to 10.9%.

## The Safe Harbor Rule

To avoid underpayment penalties, you must pay the **lesser of**:
1. 90% of current year's tax liability, OR
2. 100% of prior year's tax liability (110% if AGI > $150,000)

## Practical Savings Strategy

### Month 1-3: Build Your Buffer
Set aside **30% of net profit** into a separate tax savings account. This creates a cushion while you learn your actual tax rate.

### Quarter 1 Payment (April 15)
Pay your first estimated tax payment based on:
- Prior year's tax liability ÷ 4, OR
- 25% of projected current year tax

### Adjust as You Learn
After your first year, you'll know your effective tax rate. Most clients find they can reduce their savings rate to 25-28% once they have data.

## Industry-Specific Considerations

### Service Businesses (Consulting, Coaching)
- Higher profit margins = higher tax rates
- Set aside 30-35% to be safe

### Product-Based Businesses (E-commerce, Retail)
- Lower margins but more deductions
- 25-28% is usually sufficient

### Real Estate Investors
- Depreciation and cost segregation can significantly reduce taxable income
- Work with a specialist to optimize

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Mixing Business and Personal Funds
Open a separate savings account for taxes. Treat it as untouchable until tax time.

### 2. Forgetting State Taxes
Many business owners only calculate federal tax and get surprised by state obligations.

### 3. Not Adjusting for Growth
If your income doubles mid-year, your tax rate may jump to a higher bracket. Recalculate quarterly.

### 4. Ignoring Deductions
The more legitimate deductions you track, the lower your taxable income. Common deductions include:
- Home office (if you qualify)
- Vehicle expenses (mileage or actual)
- Software and subscriptions
- Professional development
- Health insurance premiums (self-employed)

## Real Example: Service Business Owner

**Scenario:**
- Net profit: $120,000
- Filing status: Single
- State: New York

**Tax Breakdown:**
- Self-employment tax: ~$17,000
- Federal income tax: ~$18,000
- NY state tax: ~$6,500
- **Total: ~$41,500 (35% of net profit)**

**Our Recommendation:**
Set aside 35% monthly. After first year, if actual rate is 32%, adjust to 33% for safety margin.

## Action Steps

1. **Open a separate tax savings account** today
2. **Calculate your estimated quarterly payments** using IRS Form 1040-ES
3. **Set up automatic transfers** of 30% of net profit to your tax account
4. **Track deductions religiously** using accounting software
5. **Review quarterly** with a tax professional to adjust as needed

## The Bottom Line

**Start with 30%, adjust with data, and never skip quarterly payments.**

The penalty for underpayment is typically 5-8% annually—far more expensive than setting aside too much and getting a refund.
