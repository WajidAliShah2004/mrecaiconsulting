# Does Your General Liability Policy Cover AI Mistakes? (Spoiler: Probably Not)

## The Scenario: The "Helpful" Chatbot

Imagine this: You install a customer service AI agent on your website to save money. It works great for three months, answering questions 24/7 and freeing up your team.

Then one day, it "hallucinates" and promises a client a 50% discount you can't honor. Or worse, it gives incorrect advice that causes the client financial loss.

The client sues for damages. You call your insurance broker, confident your "General Liability" policy has your back.

The broker tells you: **"Sorry, that's an excluded peril."**

## The Gap: Physical Injury vs. Financial Injury

### What General Liability Actually Covers

General Liability (GL) insurance was designed for traditional business risks:

- **Bodily injury**: Someone slips and falls in your office
- **Property damage**: You accidentally break a client's laptop
- **Advertising injury**: You're accused of copyright infringement in your marketing

### The AI Problem

AI rarely causes physical harm. Instead, AI causes:

- **Financial injury**: Bad advice leads to monetary loss
- **Reputational harm**: Incorrect information damages credibility
- **Data breaches**: Sensitive information is exposed
- **Professional negligence**: Automated services fail to meet standards

Standard GL policies were written for brick-and-mortar risks, not algorithmic risks.

**If your policy doesn't explicitly state "Technology Errors & Omissions," you are likely exposed.**

## Three Risks Your Current Policy Likely Ignores

### Risk 1: Copyright Infringement (The "Midjourney" Risk)

**Scenario**: Your marketing team uses AI to generate images or copy for your website. Unknowingly, the AI creates content that infringes on someone's trademark or copyright.

**The Problem**: Standard advertising injury coverage might deny the claim if "AI-generated content" is excluded.

**Real Example**: A company used AI to generate a logo that was nearly identical to a competitor's trademarked design. The lawsuit cost $150,000 to settle.

### Risk 2: Hallucinations & Errors (The "Bad Advice" Risk)

**Scenario**: Your AI tax bot gives a user wrong filing instructions. They follow the advice and get audited by the IRS, resulting in penalties.

**The Problem**: This is professional negligence. Without Tech E&O coverage, you're paying that settlement out of pocket.

**Real Example**: An AI-powered financial advisor recommended an investment strategy that violated SEC regulations. The firm faced a $500,000 lawsuit.

### Risk 3: Data Leakage (The "Privacy" Risk)

**Scenario**: Your AI tool accidentally exposes private client data during a system malfunction.

**The Problem**: This isn't a GL issue—it's a Cyber Liability issue. If you don't have cyber coverage, you're paying for:
- Legal fees
- Client notification costs
- Credit monitoring services
- Regulatory fines
- Reputation management

**Real Example**: A healthcare AI system exposed 10,000 patient records. The total cost including fines and settlements: $2.3 million.

## The Solution: Technology Errors & Omissions (Tech E&O)

### What It Is

Tech E&O is essentially "malpractice insurance for code." It covers:

- **Software failure**: Your AI system crashes and causes client loss
- **Breach of warranty**: Your AI doesn't perform as promised
- **Negligence in professional services**: Including automated ones
- **Data loss**: Information is corrupted or lost
- **Failure to deliver**: Your AI project doesn't meet specifications

### What It Costs

Typical Tech E&O policies for small businesses:

- **$1M coverage**: $1,500-$3,000/year
- **$2M coverage**: $2,500-$5,000/year
- **$5M coverage**: $5,000-$10,000/year

Compare that to the cost of a single lawsuit (average: $54,000 just in legal fees).

## Real-World Coverage Gaps

### Gap 1: The "Software Exclusion"

Many GL policies contain language like: *"This policy does not cover damages arising from software errors or algorithmic decision-making."*

**Translation**: Your AI is explicitly excluded.

### Gap 2: The "Professional Services" Exclusion

If you provide advice (consulting, accounting, legal), your GL policy likely excludes professional services.

**Translation**: If your AI gives advice, it's not covered.

### Gap 3: The "Cyber" Exclusion

Most GL policies now exclude cyber-related claims entirely.

**Translation**: Any data breach or privacy violation is not covered.

## Action Steps

### Step 1: Review Your Current Policy

Look for these exclusions:
- Software errors
- Professional services
- Cyber incidents
- Technology failures

### Step 2: Assess Your AI Usage

Document:
- What AI tools you use
- What data they process
- What advice they give
- What decisions they make

### Step 3: Get Proper Coverage

At minimum, you need:
- **Tech E&O**: If you use AI in your business operations
- **Cyber Liability**: If you store any client data
- **Professional Liability**: If your AI gives advice

### Step 4: Implement Governance

Create policies for:
- Approved AI tools
- Data handling procedures
- Employee training
- Incident response
